Damped Pendulum Simulator

Integrate the nonlinear damped pendulum with five classical time-stepping schemes - explicit and implicit, one-step and multistep - and watch how the choice of method and step size changes the motion.

This tool integrates the nonlinear damped pendulum

\(\ddot{\theta} + \dfrac{b}{mL}\dot{\theta} + \dfrac{g}{L}\sin\theta = 0, \quad \theta(0)=\theta_0,\quad \dot{\theta}(0)=\omega_0\)

where \(L\) is the rod length, \(m\) the bob mass, \(b\) the damping coefficient, and \(g=9.81\,\text{m/s}^2\). Writing \(\omega=\dot\theta\), \(\gamma = b/(mL)\) and \(\omega_n^2 = g/L\) gives the first-order system

\(\mathbf{y}' = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{y}),\qquad \mathbf{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\theta\\ \omega\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{y}) = \begin{pmatrix}\omega\\ -\gamma\,\omega - \omega_n^2\sin\theta\end{pmatrix}\)

Damping Regimes (linearised, \(\sin\theta\approx\theta\))

The characteristic equation \(\lambda^2+\gamma\lambda+\omega_n^2=0\) has discriminant \(\Delta = \gamma^2 - 4\omega_n^2\) and damping ratio \(\zeta = \gamma/(2\omega_n)\):

  • Underdamped \((\Delta < 0,\;\zeta<1)\): decaying oscillations, a spiral in phase space.
  • Critically damped \((\Delta = 0,\;\zeta=1)\): fastest return without oscillation.
  • Overdamped \((\Delta > 0,\;\zeta>1)\): two negative real roots, slow monotone return. When \(\gamma \gg \omega_n\) the roots are widely separated (\(\lambda_1\approx-\gamma\), \(\lambda_2\approx-\omega_n^2/\gamma\)) and the problem is stiff.

Phase Space

The phase portrait plots \(\omega = \dot{\theta}\) against \(\theta\) wrapped to \((-\pi,\pi]\). Fixed points: stable centers/spirals at \(\theta = 0\) and unstable saddles at \(\theta = \pm\pi\).

Numerical Methods

Each method advances \(\mathbf{y}_n \approx \mathbf{y}(t_n)\) with a fixed step \(h=\Delta t\). The implicit methods solve their nonlinear equation for \(\mathbf{y}_{n+1}\) by Newton's method using the exact Jacobian \(J = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1\\ -\omega_n^2\cos\theta & -\gamma\end{pmatrix}\).

  • Forward Euler (explicit, order 1): \(\mathbf{y}_{n+1} = \mathbf{y}_n + h\,\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{y}_n)\). Conditionally stable; for an undamped pendulum it always pumps energy in, so the swing slowly grows.
  • Backward Euler (implicit, order 1): \(\mathbf{y}_{n+1} = \mathbf{y}_n + h\,\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{y}_{n+1})\). L-stable - stable for any step on a decaying problem - but adds artificial damping.
  • RK4 (explicit, order 4): the classical four-stage Runge–Kutta method. Very accurate for moderate steps, but explicit, so it becomes unstable on stiff problems unless \(h\) is small.
  • Trapezoidal (implicit, order 2, Crank–Nicolson): \(\mathbf{y}_{n+1} = \mathbf{y}_n + \tfrac{h}{2}\big[\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{y}_n)+\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{y}_{n+1})\big]\). A-stable and preserves the amplitude of undamped linear oscillations exactly, but not L-stable: very stiff components are not damped and can flip sign every step.
  • BDF2 (implicit two-step, order 2): \(\mathbf{y}_{n+1} - \tfrac{4}{3}\mathbf{y}_n + \tfrac{1}{3}\mathbf{y}_{n-1} = \tfrac{2}{3}h\,\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{y}_{n+1})\). A- and L-stable, the workhorse for stiff problems. Its first step is taken with backward Euler.

Inputs accept expressions such as pi/4, 2*pi, sqrt(2).

Numerical accuracy - global error vs the exact solution

Each integrator is run on the linear (small-angle) damped-pendulum test problem, whose exact solution is known in closed form, using the tool's own parameters and time step. The reported value is the largest angular deviation over the run relative to the peak amplitude - lower is better, and for small steps the ordering follows each method's order of accuracy.
