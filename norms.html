Vector & Matrix Norms

Explore $\ell_1$, $\ell_2$, $\ell_p$, and $\ell_\infty$ norms with step-by-step algebra and geometric unit-ball visualizations.

A norm $\|\cdot\|$ measures the "size" of a vector or matrix. Different norms weigh the components differently, producing different geometric shapes for the unit ball $\{\mathbf{x}: \|\mathbf{x}\|\le 1\}$.

  • $\ell_1$ norm (Manhattan): $\|x\|_1 = |x_1|+|x_2|$ — unit ball is a diamond
  • $\ell_2$ norm (Euclidean): $\|x\|_2 = \sqrt{x_1^2+x_2^2}$ — unit ball is a disk
  • $\ell_p$ norm: $\|x\|_p = (|x_1|^p+|x_2|^p)^{1/p}$ — unit ball is a superellipse
  • $\ell_\infty$ norm (Chebyshev): $\|x\|_\infty = \max(|x_1|,|x_2|)$ — unit ball is a square

As $p$ increases from $1$ to $\infty$, the unit ball morphs continuously from the diamond through the disk to the square. For matrices, the induced norm $\|A\|_p = \max_{\|x\|_p=1}\|Ax\|_p$ measures maximum stretching.
