Change of Coordinates Matrix Calculator

Given two bases \(\mathcal{V} = \{v_1, \ldots, v_n\}\) and \(\mathcal{U} = \{u_1, \ldots, u_n\}\) for \(\mathbb{R}^n\), compute the change of coordinates matrix1 \(\mathbf{S}\) that converts coordinates from basis \(\mathcal{V}\) to basis \(\mathcal{U}\).
Supports complex number entries and displays results as fractions or decimals.

Want to see how change of basis works geometrically? Try the Coordinate Transform Visualizer for an interactive animated demonstration.

The matrix \(\mathbf{V}\) has columns \(v_1, \ldots, v_n\) (initial basis vectors) and \(\mathbf{U}\) has columns \(u_1, \ldots, u_n\) (final basis vectors).

Change of Coordinates Matrix:2

\( \mathbf{S} = \mathbf{U}^{-1} \mathbf{V} \)

If \([\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{V}}\) represents coordinate vectors3 in basis \(\mathcal{V}\), then:

\( [\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{U}} = \mathbf{S} \, [\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{V}} \)

Key Ideas:

  • \(\mathbf{S}\) transforms coordinate vectors from basis \(\mathcal{V}\) to basis \(\mathcal{U}\)
  • The inverse \(\mathbf{S}^{-1} = \mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{U}\) goes from \(\mathcal{U}\) back to \(\mathcal{V}\)
  • If \(\mathcal{U}\) is the standard basis, then \(\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{V}\) converts to standard coordinates
  • Change of basis is fundamental in applications4 such as diagonalization, principal component analysis, and coordinate transforms in computer graphics

Footnotes

  1. A change of basis (or change of coordinates) expresses the same vector in a different coordinate system. Given two bases for the same vector space, there exists a unique invertible matrix that converts coordinates from one basis to the other. ↑
  2. The transition matrix (or change of coordinates matrix) \(\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{U}^{-1}\mathbf{V}\) is computed by row-reducing the augmented matrix \([\mathbf{U} \mid \mathbf{V}]\) to \([\mathbf{I} \mid \mathbf{S}]\). It is invertible whenever both bases are valid (i.e., both \(\mathbf{U}\) and \(\mathbf{V}\) are invertible). ↑
  3. A coordinate vector \([\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}}\) lists the scalars \(c_1, \ldots, c_n\) such that \(\mathbf{x} = c_1 b_1 + \cdots + c_n b_n\) relative to the basis \(\mathcal{B} = \{b_1, \ldots, b_n\}\). Different bases yield different coordinate vectors for the same geometric vector. ↑
  4. Applications of change of basis include: diagonalization of matrices (\(A = PDP^{-1}\)), principal component analysis (PCA) in data science, coordinate transforms in computer graphics and robotics, and simplifying linear operators by choosing a basis of eigenvectors. ↑
