Cauchy Principal Value Integral Family

Five canonical families of principal value integrals — move the pole, watch the symmetric limit converge, and compare against the exact closed form.

An integrand with a simple pole at an interior point $x_0$ — a factor like $\dfrac{1}{x-x_0}$ — is not Lebesgue integrable there: the one-sided pieces diverge to $+\infty$ and $-\infty$. The Cauchy principal value rescues the integral by deleting a symmetric neighborhood of the pole and shrinking it to zero, so the two infinities cancel exactly:

$$\operatorname{PV}\!\int_a^b \frac{g(x)}{x-x_0}\,dx \;=\; \lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^+}\left(\int_a^{x_0-\varepsilon}+\int_{x_0+\varepsilon}^{b}\right)\frac{g(x)}{x-x_0}\,dx.$$

The symmetry is essential: an asymmetric deletion (say $[x_0-\varepsilon,\,x_0+2\varepsilon]$) leaves a residual $g(x_0)\ln 2$ and gives a different answer. The principal value is the unique choice that keeps the log-divergences balanced.

How the numbers below are computed

  • Symmetric limit $S(\varepsilon)=\int_a^{x_0-\varepsilon}+\int_{x_0+\varepsilon}^{b}$: computed directly by adaptive Simpson on the two deleted pieces. As $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$ the left piece $L(\varepsilon)$ and right piece $R(\varepsilon)$ each blow up like $\mp\,g(x_0)\ln\varepsilon$, yet $S(\varepsilon)=L+R$ converges.
  • Principal value (the headline number): computed by subtracting the singularity, $\operatorname{PV}\!\int \dfrac{g(x)}{x-x_0} = \int\Big[\dfrac{g(x)}{x-x_0}-\dfrac{r}{x-x_0}\Big]dx + r\,\ln\dfrac{b-x_0}{x_0-a}$, where $r=g(x_0)$ is the residue. The bracketed integrand has a removable singularity, so ordinary quadrature is accurate to machine precision.
  • Exact: every family here has a known closed form (residue theorem, series, or a classical special function), shown for comparison.

Why it matters

Principal value integrals are the backbone of Hilbert transforms, Kramers–Kronig dispersion relations, airfoil / thin-wing theory, and boundary integral equations. Three of the five families below are exactly these transforms.
