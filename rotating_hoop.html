Bead on a Rotating Hoop

A bead sliding on a wire hoop spun about a vertical axis - the textbook centrifugal pitchfork bifurcation. Watch the effective potential split into a double well as the spin rate \(\omega\) crosses \(\omega_c=\sqrt{g/R}\). Friction, parametric resonance, and a choice of symplectic energy-preserving integrators included.

A bead of mass \(m\) slides on a circular wire hoop of radius \(R\) that is forced to rotate about its vertical diameter at angular speed \(\omega\). The bead's location is the angle \(\theta\) measured from the bottom of the hoop. Its height is \(z=-R\cos\theta\) and its distance from the spin axis is \(\rho=R\sin\theta\), so the bead carries an azimuthal speed \(\rho\omega = R\omega\sin\theta\) in addition to its motion \(R\dot\theta\) along the wire.

Lagrangian

With the rotation \(\omega\) prescribed by the motor, \(\theta\) is the single degree of freedom:

\[\mathcal{L} = \tfrac{1}{2}mR^2\dot\theta^2 + \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}mR^2\omega^2\sin^2\theta}_{\text{rotational KE}} + mgR\cos\theta\]

The Euler–Lagrange equation, with a viscous friction torque \(-mR^2\gamma\,\dot\theta\) from the bead rubbing on the wire, gives the equation of motion:

\[\ddot\theta = \omega^2\sin\theta\cos\theta - \dfrac{g}{R}\sin\theta - \gamma\,\dot\theta\]

Effective potential & the pitchfork

The centrifugal term acts like a velocity-free contribution to the potential. Writing \(V_{\text{eff}}(\theta) = -\tfrac{1}{2}mR^2\omega^2\sin^2\theta - mgR\cos\theta\), equilibria solve \(V_{\text{eff}}'(\theta)=0\):

  • \(\theta=0\) (bottom) is stable while \(\omega<\omega_c\) and becomes unstable above it.
  • For \(\omega>\omega_c=\sqrt{g/R}\) two new stable equilibria appear at \(\theta^\ast=\pm\arccos\!\big(g/(R\omega^2)\big)\) - a supercritical pitchfork bifurcation. The bead climbs the hoop as the spin increases, exactly as a centrifugal governor does.
  • Small-oscillation frequencies: \(\omega_n^2=g/R-\omega^2\) about the bottom, and \(\omega_n^2=\omega^2-\omega_c^4/\omega^2\) about a tilted well.

Hamiltonian (Jacobi integral)

With \(p_\theta=mR^2\dot\theta\) and constant \(\omega\), the energy function is conserved when \(\gamma=0\):

\[H = \dfrac{p_\theta^2}{2mR^2} + V_{\text{eff}}(\theta) = \dfrac{p_\theta^2}{2mR^2} - \tfrac{1}{2}mR^2\omega^2\sin^2\theta - mgR\cos\theta\]

This Jacobi integral is not the bead's total mechanical energy - the motor enforcing the rotation does work. The live readout tracks the drift \(\Delta H\); set \(\gamma=0\) and modulation \(A=0\) to make it a clean test of the integrator's energy preservation.

Friction & resonance

  • Friction \(\gamma\): drains energy so the bead relaxes onto a stable equilibrium. Large \(\gamma\) gives the overdamped Strogatz limit \(\gamma\dot\theta \approx \omega^2\sin\theta\cos\theta - (g/R)\sin\theta\) - a stiff regime.
  • Parametric resonance: modulating the spin, \(\omega(t)=\omega_0\big(1+A\sin\Omega t\big)\), pumps the centrifugal term. When the drive frequency \(\Omega\approx 2\omega_n\), oscillations about a well grow without a direct push - the same mechanism as a child pumping a swing.

Numerical methods

  • RK4 - classical 4th-order Runge–Kutta. Very accurate per step (the smallest \(\Delta H\) here at \(\Delta t=0.004\)), but not symplectic: its energy error carries no guarantee of staying bounded and can accumulate over very long runs, near separatrices, or at larger steps. Its bounded stability region also forces a small step on stiff (strongly overdamped, large \(\gamma\)) problems - the regime where the implicit method earns its keep.
  • Symplectic 4th-order (Forest–Ruth) - a symmetric composition of three velocity-Verlet substeps (Yoshida's construction). Symplectic and time-reversible like plain Verlet, so the \(\Delta H\) error stays bounded (oscillates rather than drifts) for all time, but 4th-order accurate: its energy-error amplitude is roughly \(10^4\times\) smaller than 2nd-order Verlet at the same step, on par with RK4 while preserving phase-space structure.
  • Implicit midpoint - symplectic and A-stable. It preserves the Jacobi integral to near machine precision and, crucially, stays stable on the stiff overdamped / fast-spin regimes where explicit methods would need a tiny step. Solved here by fixed-point iteration of \(y_{n+1}=y_n+h\,f\!\big(\tfrac{y_n+y_{n+1}}{2}\big)\).
