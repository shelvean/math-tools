Lorenz System

The Lorenz System

The Lorenz system is a set of three coupled ordinary differential equations originally derived from atmospheric convection models. It is one of the earliest examples of deterministic chaos.

Parameters

  • \(\sigma\) — the Prandtl number (ratio of momentum to thermal diffusivity)
  • \(\rho\) — the Rayleigh number (driving force of convection)
  • \(\beta\) — geometric factor of the convection cell

Key Features

  • For \(\rho < 1\), the origin is a stable equilibrium
  • For \(1 < \rho < 24.74\), two non-trivial equilibria appear (stable spirals)
  • For \(\rho \approx 24.74\), chaotic behavior emerges (the famous "butterfly" attractor)
  • Sensitive dependence: Tiny changes in initial conditions lead to wildly different trajectories

Try It

  • Use "Animate Rho" to sweep through different regimes
  • Launch particles to observe sensitive dependence on initial conditions
  • Use "Transient Chaos" at \(\rho = 22\) to see chaotic transients before settling
\[ \begin{cases} \dot{x} = \sigma(y - x) \\ \dot{y} = x(\rho - z) - y \\ \dot{z} = xy - \beta z \end{cases} \]
