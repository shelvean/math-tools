Elastic (Spring) Pendulum Simulation

The elastic pendulum (also called the spring pendulum or swinging spring) is a mass attached to a spring that is free to both stretch and swing. It is one of the simplest systems exhibiting two coupled degrees of freedom and nonlinear energy exchange.

Unlike a rigid pendulum (fixed length) or a simple spring-mass (purely vertical), the elastic pendulum combines radial oscillation (stretching) with angular oscillation (swinging), producing rich dynamics including quasi-periodic orbits, energy transfer between modes, and even chaos for large amplitudes.

Equations of Motion

Using polar coordinates \((r, \theta)\) where \(r\) is the spring length and \(\theta\) is the angle from the vertical, the Lagrangian is:

\(\mathcal{L} = \tfrac{1}{2}m(\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\theta}^2) + mgr\cos\theta - \tfrac{1}{2}k(r - L_0)^2\)

Applying the Euler–Lagrange equations yields:

\(\ddot{r} = r\dot{\theta}^2 + g\cos\theta - \dfrac{k}{m}(r - L_0) - \dfrac{b}{m}\dot{r}\)

\(\ddot{\theta} = -\dfrac{2\dot{r}\dot{\theta}}{r} - \dfrac{g}{r}\sin\theta - \dfrac{b}{m}\dot{\theta}\)

where \(b\) is a damping coefficient applied to both radial and angular velocities.

Observable Phenomena

  • Energy transfer: Energy periodically shifts between radial (spring) and angular (pendulum) modes, especially near the 2:1 frequency ratio
  • Autoparametric resonance: When \(\omega_r \approx 2\omega_\theta\), the radial mode parametrically excites the angular mode
  • Quasi-periodic motion: The mass traces intricate Lissajous-like patterns in 2D space
  • Chaotic behavior: Large amplitudes or specific parameter ratios can lead to sensitive dependence on initial conditions

Characteristic Frequencies

Radial (spring): \(\omega_r = \sqrt{k/m}\)
Angular (pendulum): \(\omega_\theta = \sqrt{g/L_{\text{eq}}}\) where \(L_{\text{eq}} = L_0 + mg/k\)

Numerical Method

The simulation integrates the coupled 4D system \((r, \dot{r}, \theta, \dot{\theta})\) using the classical 4th-order Runge-Kutta (RK4) method, providing excellent accuracy for nonlinear oscillatory dynamics.

\[ \ddot{r} = r\dot{\theta}^2 + g\cos\theta - \frac{k}{m}(r - L_0) - \frac{b}{m}\dot{r}, \qquad \ddot{\theta} = -\frac{2\dot{r}\dot{\theta}}{r} - \frac{g}{r}\sin\theta - \frac{b}{m}\dot{\theta} \]

About This Simulation

The elastic pendulum (spring pendulum) combines a mass-spring system with a simple pendulum, yielding two coupled degrees of freedom: the spring length \(r\) and the swing angle \(\theta\).

\(\mathcal{L} = \tfrac{1}{2}m(\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\theta}^2) + mgr\cos\theta - \tfrac{1}{2}k(r - L_0)^2\)

Key phenomena to observe:

  • Energy transfer between radial and angular modes
  • Autoparametric resonance at \(\omega_r \approx 2\omega_\theta\)
  • Quasi-periodic orbits producing Lissajous-like 2D trajectories
  • Chaotic motion at large amplitudes

Numerical method: The simulation integrates the coupled 4D system using the classical 4th-order Runge-Kutta (RK4) method.
