Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors Calculator

Compute the eigenvalues1 and eigenvectors2 of a square matrix \(A\).
Supports real, complex, fractions (e.g. 3+2i, 1/2), and expressions like pi, sqrt(2), exp(1).

Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors:

  • For a square matrix \(A\), an eigenvalue \(\lambda\) and corresponding eigenvector \(\mathbf{v}\) satisfy \(A\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v}\)
  • The eigenvector \(\mathbf{v}\) is a direction that gets scaled by factor \(\lambda\) when transformed by \(A\)
  • Eigenvalues are found by solving the characteristic equation3 \(\det(A - \lambda I) = 0\)
  • A matrix may have real or complex eigenvalues; complex eigenvalues of real matrices come in conjugate pairs

Generalized Eigenvectors:

  • When a matrix is defective4 (lacks enough eigenvectors to span the space), generalized eigenvectors \(\mathbf{w}\) satisfy \((A - \lambda I)\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{v}\) where \(\mathbf{v}\) is an eigenvector (Jordan chain condition)
  • Generalized eigenvectors complete the basis when geometric multiplicity is less than algebraic multiplicity

This calculator finds eigenvalues with algebraic multiplicities, eigenvectors scaled for clean display (surd form, fractions), and generalized eigenvectors when needed. Complex eigenvectors are displayed in \(a + bi\) form.

Footnotes

  1. An eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of a square matrix \(A\) is a scalar such that there exists a nonzero vector \(\mathbf{v}\) with \(A\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v}\). The set of all eigenvalues is called the spectrum of \(A\). ↑
  2. An eigenvector corresponding to eigenvalue \(\lambda\) is any nonzero vector \(\mathbf{v}\) in the null space of \(A - \lambda I\). Eigenvectors are unique only up to scalar multiples. ↑
  3. The characteristic equation \(\det(A - \lambda I) = 0\) is a polynomial of degree \(n\) in \(\lambda\). By the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, it always has \(n\) roots (counted with multiplicity) in \(\mathbb{C}\). ↑
  4. A matrix is defective if its geometric multiplicity (dimension of the eigenspace) is strictly less than its algebraic multiplicity (multiplicity as a root of the characteristic polynomial) for at least one eigenvalue. Such matrices cannot be diagonalized. ↑
